Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026. This policy covers the Screenshot to Calendar app and its backend service, and explains what personal data we process, why, for how long, and the rights you have under UK GDPR.

Who is responsible for your data

The data controller is Rhys Dunne. For any privacy question or to exercise a right below, contact rhys.dunne@gmail.com.

What we store

  • Your Google account identity: email address and Google account ID, obtained when you sign in with Google.
  • A Google Calendar refresh token, encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM), used solely to create and update calendar events you ask for.
  • Images you share into the app and the event data extracted from them. These are stored encrypted at rest in AWS (eu-west-2, London); see "International transfers" below for where they are processed.
  • Corrections you make to extracted event data.
  • Your settings: the calendar you add events to, your timezone, and your "Help improve extraction" choice. The app takes the timezone from your device when you set it up, and you can change it in Settings. We use it to read event dates and times. If it is Europe/London, we also add "London" to venue lookups.
  • Your email address on our sign-up list, if you were invited. Sign-up is invite-only, and the list holds the Google email addresses allowed to create an account. Deleting your account does not remove you from the list; ask us and we will.

How images are processed

To turn a screenshot into an event, the image is sent to Anthropic's Claude API to classify it and extract the event details, and venue names may be sent to Google's Places API to resolve addresses. Images are used only to provide the service. Extraction is automated; low-confidence results are held for you to review and approve in the app before any calendar event is created.

Our lawful basis (UK GDPR Article 6)

  • Performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)) for the core service: storing your images, extracting event details, and creating the calendar events you request.
  • Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) for the optional "Help improve extraction" use described below. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) for keeping the service secure and preventing abuse.

Improving the product (opt-in only)

If — and only if — you enable "Help improve extraction" in Settings, the corrections you make may be used to improve the extraction prompt. A correction records which detail you changed, and its old and new value. We look for mistakes that recur across at least three different captures, and send those corrections (not your images) to Anthropic's Claude to draft a better prompt. This is off by default, and the consent state is recorded with each correction at the time you make it. Turning it off stops any future correction from being used. Corrections on a capture you have deleted are not used.

Your images never go into our evaluation dataset. We test prompt changes against that dataset, and it is published in the app's public code repository, so it is built only from the developer's own screenshots and generated test images. It is never built from other users' data, even with this setting on.

International transfers

Your data is stored in the UK (AWS London, eu-west-2). However, when an image is processed it is transmitted to Anthropic in the United States, and venue lookups and calendar operations involve Google. These transfers outside the UK are covered by the UK International Data Transfer Agreement / Standard Contractual Clauses in our agreements with those providers.

Sub-processors

  • Anthropic (US) — image classification and event extraction, and drafting prompt changes from opted-in corrections.
  • Google — sign-in (OAuth), Calendar, and Places (venue → address).
  • Amazon Web Services (UK, eu-west-2) — hosting and storage.

How long we keep it

  • Your account, images, events, and corrections are kept for as long as your account exists. You can delete any capture, or your whole account, at any time (see "Your rights"). Deleting a capture removes its image and its record. The corrections you made to it stay with your account, and are no longer used to improve the prompt, until you delete your account.
  • Operational AI-usage logs (token and cost records, no image content) are automatically deleted after 90 days.
  • Data exports you generate expire after 7 days.
  • Service logs record internal account and capture IDs, but not your images or event details, and are deleted after 30 days.
  • Backups: our database keeps rolling backups for 35 days. Data you delete stays in them until it ages out, at most 35 days later. We restore a backup only to recover from a failure. If we ever do, we will delete again, by hand, any account deleted since that backup was taken. This is a best-effort step, not an automatic one. Images are not backed up, so a deleted image is gone at once.

Automated processing

Event extraction is automated, but you remain in control: results you can act on are shown in the app, low-confidence extractions wait for your review and approval, and you can edit or delete any event. Because creating a calendar entry has no legal or similarly significant effect and you review the outcome, this is not solely-automated decision-making under Article 22.

Your rights (UK GDPR)

  • Access & portability: Settings → Export my data produces a machine-readable copy of everything stored about you, including links to your source images.
  • Erasure: Settings → Delete account deletes your images, your data exports, and every record stored for your account (profile, captures, corrections, and AI-usage records), and signs you out everywhere. It also asks Google to revoke the app's access to your Google account. That request is best-effort: if it fails, deletion still goes ahead, and you can remove the app's access yourself in your Google Account settings. Events already added to your Google Calendar stay there. Backups age out as described above.
  • Rectification: correct extracted details in the app at any time.
  • Objection, restriction, and withdrawal of consent: contact us, or toggle "Help improve extraction" off, at any time.
  • Complaint: you can lodge a complaint with the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at ico.org.uk.

What we never do

We do not sell data, run third-party ads or analytics, or read calendar events other than in the date window checked for duplicates in the calendar you selected.
